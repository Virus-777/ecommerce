# Fundamentals, not flair, win ecommerce UX

Independent 2025–2026 audits give their top UX ratings to focused retailers, not to the biggest marketplaces. **Chewy, Nordstrom, Adidas, Nike, Hoka, Everlane, bol.com and Chrono24** hold Baymard top-1% awards or labels. **Amazon** leads on traffic (about 1.3B monthly unique visitors) and is near the top of customer satisfaction (ACSI 83 in 2025), yet it has no Baymard award. Its lead comes from selection, delivery and familiarity rather than interface craft. The leaders feel simple because they get right the basics that most sites still miss: guest checkout, delivery shown as a date, shipping cost and return policy on the product page, size buttons, search that tolerates typos, visible filters and scope, and review photos users can swipe through. Baymard rates **75% of mobile sites "mediocre"**, so doing these basics well already sets a store apart. For a hybrid marketplace like Marketly, the "smart" layer is making many sellers easy to read. That means a readable "Sold by / Ships from" line under the buy button, two separate badge systems (who the seller is, and how well they perform), a cart grouped by seller with one payment, and product reviews kept apart from seller feedback. For the pages Marketly has not designed yet, the evidence is strong in some places and thin in others. It is strong for the reviews page, address entry, backup-code sign-in (NIST SP 800-63B-4, final July 2025) and the back-office tables, forms, audit logs and risk reviews (documented in Shopify, GitHub Primer, IBM Carbon, Stripe and GitHub docs). It is weak for coupon eligible-items pages, storefront "see all" pages and help articles. **Shopee, Mercado Libre, Rakuten and Target Plus have no independent UX scores at all.** Restraint is now a legal matter as well as a design choice: in June 2026 Italy fined an e-retailer EUR 2M for fake countdown timers and scarcity counters.

## Chewy and Nordstrom lead when audits, surveys and traffic agree

No source ranks "best ecommerce UX" across marketplaces and brand stores in one list, so the shortlist below combines three kinds of signal that measure different things. **Baymard** scores UX directly: it hand-audits each site against 500+ guidelines and gives awards to the top 1% of an industry or theme. Its 2025 awards went to 51 winners picked from 400+ candidates, based on more than 275,000 weighted scores ([Baymard UX Awards 2025, Dec 2025](https://baymard.com/research-articles/ux-awards-2025)). **ACSI and Forrester** measure overall satisfaction, which includes price, delivery and service as well as the website. **Similarweb** measures popularity only. A site that scores well on all three is the safest model to copy. A site that scores well on only one is a model for that one thing.

The table points to an uncomfortable conclusion for anyone copying "the big players". **The sites with the most traffic are popular mainly because of selection, price and logistics, not because of top-1% UX.** Amazon, Walmart, eBay, Etsy and Temu were all benchmarked or eligible and none reached the top 1% ([Baymard UX Awards 2025](https://baymard.com/research-articles/ux-awards-2025)). Chewy is the only name that Baymard, ACSI and Forrester all rate at the top. Nordstrom is the strongest example of a first-party store running alongside a marketplace, which is the model Marketly is building. The case against copying Amazon has a reasonable counter-argument: shoppers have learned Amazon's conventions over decades, so matching them lowers learning cost. Marketly should therefore borrow Amazon's *vocabulary* ("Sold by", "Ships from", "Deliver to", "Clip coupon") while taking its *execution* from the Baymard winners.

Two popular kinds of evidence turn out to be weak. First, design awards hardly touch mainstream shopping. Apple's 2025 App Store Awards and Google Play's Best of 2025 named no shopping app ([Apple Newsroom, Dec 2025](https://apple.com/newsroom/2025/12/apple-unveils-the-winners-of-the-2025-app-store-awards); [Google, 2025](https://blog.google/products/google-play/best-apps-games-2025/)). The 2026 Webby for Shopping & Retail apps went to Apple Pay, a payment product ([MacTech, Apr 2026](https://www.mactech.com/2026/04/21/apple-wins-three-webby-awards-from-the-international-academy-of-digital-arts-sciences/amp/)). Awwwards e-commerce honours go to small, visually led brand sites such as Oura Ring ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration_search/winner_category_ecommerce/)). These awards reward visual craft; Baymard rewards how efficiently shoppers complete tasks. Second, **app store stars contradict satisfaction surveys**. Temu and Shein both show 4.7 on the App Store ([unstar.app, low weight](https://unstar.app/de/compare/temu-vs-shein)), while Temu has the lowest ACSI score among the major online retailers. Marketly should not use star ratings as a measure of UX quality.

Several brand stores often cited as design references (IKEA, Allbirds, Glossier, Warby Parker, Gymshark, Patagonia, Sephora) have **no 2025–2026 independent award or benchmark ranking**. Their reputations come mostly from listicles. Baymard's free material does credit some of their specific patterns. Allbirds' clickable "Shoes" menu header shows all shoes before the shopper picks a subcategory ([Baymard Navigation 2025](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice)). IKEA UK shows every fulfilment option side by side at checkout ([Baymard Checkout 2025](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-UX)). Their apps rate highly, but app ratings are a weak signal: IKEA 4.8 as of April 2026 ([AppFollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/ikea/1452164827?country=us)) and Sephora 4.9 ([App Store SG](https://apps.apple.com/sg/app/sephora-beauty-shopping/id1152899196)). Baymard's Warby Parker and Glossier case studies exist, but their scores are paywalled ([Baymard: Warby Parker](https://baymard.com/ux-benchmark/case-studies/warby-parker)).

## Simple means fixing what most sites still get wrong

The leaders do not stand out for visual flair. They stand out because they follow guidelines that most sites ignore. Baymard's 2025–2026 benchmarks rate as mediocre or worse **63–64% of web checkouts, 52–64% of product pages, 56% of on-site search experiences and 75% of mobile sites** ([Checkout, Nov 2025](https://baymard.com/research-articles/current-state-of-checkout-ux); [Product page, Mar 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux); [Search, Apr 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/collections/on-site-search); [Mobile, Jul 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/mobile-ux-ecommerce)). Only one mobile site in Baymard's 150+ site review rated "Good". Getting the basics right is therefore a real competitive advantage. Marketly's design team should treat the failure rates below as a checklist.

**Navigation.** Simple navigation comes from a shallow category tree: about 10 subcategories per level (60% of sites exceed this), the current section highlighted (95% of sites don't do this), clickable menu headers and a 300–500 ms hover delay before dropdowns open. Category landing pages should show subcategory tiles above promotions. Baymard specifically criticises Nike's "Men" page, where a featured link overshadows the subcategory links ([Baymard Navigation 2025](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice)). This matters for Marketly's Official Store pages. The "simple menu" feel of brand sites largely comes from small catalogues, and an Official Store can recreate it by limiting itself to about 10 top categories with subcategory tiles first and campaigns second. Restraint also counts. Baymard praises LEGO for keeping prime homepage space free of ads, criticises GAP's offer overlays, and says **mobile carousels should not auto-rotate** (same source).

**Product page.** The most-missed fundamentals map almost exactly onto the buy area Marketly needs. Use size and colour buttons instead of dropdowns (57% of sites use dropdowns). Show a shipping-cost estimate (67% don't). Link the return policy (44% don't, even though 60% of users look for it on the product page and 15% have abandoned an order over a poor return policy). Show unit price (81% don't). Let shoppers swipe through all review images (63% don't). Have staff reply to negative reviews (89% don't). Let guests save items without an account (89% handle saving poorly; Adidas is criticised for forcing an account to save) ([Baymard Product Page 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)). Show delivery as a date, not a speed. Baymard praises Apple for showing an actual arrival date and Best Buy for a cut-off countdown ("Order in the next 43 minutes for Tuesday delivery"), which 83% of sites lack ([Baymard Checkout 2025](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-UX)). The one notable brand number in this area comes from a vendor case study: Gymshark made its existing size guide more prominent, size-guide use rose **40%** in six weeks, and checkout rates rose 11% after related fixes ([UserTesting: Gymshark, undated](https://www.usertesting.com/resources/customers/gymshark)). The lesson is that fit help works without AR. It only needs to be easy to see.

**Search.** Baymard's 2025 search awards went to bol.com, Gamma, Albertsons and Safeway. They succeed on basics where most sites fail. 69% of sites give no autocomplete suggestions for close misspellings. Only 19% of autocomplete implementations perform well. 54% don't switch thumbnails to the variant searched for ("red dress" should show the red one). 37% don't keep the query after searching. 21% of mobile sites have no search submit button ([Baymard Search](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-search-report-and-benchmark)). In an Official Store, search should default to the store's own catalogue and offer a one-tap "search all of Marketly" fallback. This is an inference from Baymard's guidance on scoping search to categories, not a pattern tested at a named marketplace.

**Checkout.** Checkout friction is the biggest measurable lever. Baymard estimates large sites can gain up to **35%** in conversion from checkout design. **18%** of shoppers abandon because they are forced to create an account, and 62% of sites don't make guest checkout the most prominent option. Etsy is praised for doing so. Other common failures are generic error messages (94% of sites; Away is praised for specific ones), complex password rules (65%; a 6–8 character minimum is enough), text-field quantity inputs where a +/- stepper is better (97%; Walmart is praised), required and optional fields not both marked (61%), and phone numbers requested without saying why (49%; **Apple is criticised by name**) ([Baymard Checkout 2025](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-UX)). Baymard's research also shows an ideal checkout needs only about 7–8 real input fields, against a US average of about 23 form elements. That figure reached the notes through secondary summaries ([Baymard via snippet](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-checkout-usability)).

**Phone layout and the app.** Because Marketly's phone layout doubles as its Android app, one Baymard pattern matters directly. **Apps beat the web on checkout** (46% mediocre or worse against 63–64% for the web, and 7% rated "good" against 2%). **Apps lose on product pages** (64% mediocre or worse, the worst of any platform) ([Baymard Checkout](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-UX); [Baymard Product Page](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)). Apps tend to simplify paying and cut product information in the process. Marketly should keep its phone product page as complete as its desktop one, with the same delivery, returns and seller facts. The top mobile failures are also directly fixable: 94% don't highlight the current scope, 66% don't show applied filters, 42% list each variant as a separate product, and 54% have no address validation ([Baymard Mobile 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/mobile-ux-ecommerce)). A sticky add-to-cart bar on mobile product pages is standard practice, but the only supporting figures (a 7.9% average lift) come from vendors with unclear methods ([WiserReview](https://wiserreview.com/glossary/sticky-add-to-cart/)). Popularity is also shifting toward apps: ecommerce app sessions grew 13% while web visits fell 1% ([Similarweb, Sep 2025](https://ir.similarweb.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/132)).

## Marketplaces earn trust by making every seller legible

A single-brand store has one seller, one returns policy and one delivery promise. A hybrid marketplace has to make each of these facts visible per offer without cluttering the page. The marketplaces in scope have arrived at the same patterns, although the evidence for them is mostly near-primary partner documentation and secondary explainers rather than independent usability research.

**Offer and seller disclosure.** Zalando's partner documentation says customers see "sold and shipped by partner: xyz" **directly under the add-to-cart button**, and can filter to show only products shipped by Zalando ([Zalando partner Q&A](https://corporate.zalando.com/sites/default/files/media-download/Questions_Webinar1.pdf)). Amazon shows "Ships from" and "Sold by" under the buy button and puts other offers behind a "New & Used" entry. That entry opens a list to compare price, condition, delivery date and seller rating ([AMZScout, secondary](https://amzscout.net/blog/sold-by-amazon/)). Trade press has criticised Zalando's disclosure as too subtle, calling it "a little icon under the product" ([SGI Europe](https://www.sgieurope.com/zalando-expands-its-partnership-program/61587.article)). Marketly should therefore show a text line under Add to Cart, "Sold by [Seller ›] · Ships from [Marketly / Seller]". It should keep fulfilment and seller identity as separate facts, and add one secondary "N other offers from [price]" entry that opens a bottom sheet on phone. For used and refurbished goods, eBay offers the strongest model. Since its Refurbished programme, sellers outside it must list items as "used", and inside it there are four graded conditions backed by the platform, with "Certified" eligible for a two-year warranty ([SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1616483/ebay-certified-refurbished-is-it-worth-buying)). Marketly should use fixed condition grades with plain definitions rather than seller-written labels.

**Badges on two separate axes.** The best seller badges are earned from measurable service, shown consistently and explained on tap. Walmart's Pro Seller badge appears in search results, on product pages, in the additional-sellers list, on the seller profile and in the cart. On hover it promises "quality service provider, consistent on-time delivery, and free online or in-store returns" ([Marketplace Pulse](https://www.marketplacepulse.com/articles/walmart-marketplace-pro-sellers)). Walmart's own Feb–Apr 2025 data, reported by a vendor, gives Pro Sellers **1.7x** the average conversion ([Zentail](https://www.zentail.com/blog/walmart-marketplace-pro-seller-badge)). Etsy's Star Seller requires 95% response rate, 95% on-time shipping and a 4.8 average rating ([Etsy Seller Handbook](https://www.etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/476775452989)). Sellers criticise it because satisfied buyers often leave four stars, which then count against the badge ([Gelato](https://gelato.com/blog/etsy-star-seller)). Mercado Libre shows reputation as a five-colour thermometer built from claims, cancellations and late shipments, plus a separate MercadoLíder medal ([Mercado Libre developer docs](https://developers.mercadolibre.com.ar/en_us/sellers-reputation)). Shopee keeps a brand-verified "Mall" tier with an authenticity guarantee apart from its performance-based "Preferred" tag ([Shopappy, secondary](https://shopappy.com/ecommerce/shopee/shopee-mall)). For Marketly, this means **identity and performance must never merge into one badge**. "Marketly Official Store" (and optionally "Official Brand Store") says *who* is selling. A monthly earned badge for approved third parties says *how well* they sell. Each badge should open a bottom sheet explaining what it guarantees. Next to any colour indicator, show a plain percentage such as "98% on time" for accessibility.

**Cart and checkout.** The case for grouping the cart by seller is logical rather than empirical: delivery cost, delivery date and the return path each belong to one seller, and they mean nothing when averaged across the cart. Free-delivery thresholds should therefore show progress inside each seller group. The order summary should say upfront that separate packages and invoices will arrive, while giving the buyer **one order number to quote to support** ([Mercur, vendor](https://www.mercurjs.com/marketplace-academy/marketplace-checkout-several-sellers); [Mercur cart](https://www.mercurjs.com/marketplace-academy/multi-vendor-shopping-cart-marketplace)). The cheaper alternative, one checkout per vendor, trades UX simplicity for simpler payouts ([Sharetribe](https://www.sharetribe.com/academy/multivendor-shopping-cart/)). Marketly should use one combined payment. **No primary source shows how Amazon, Walmart or Shopee actually render seller groups or word price-change notices.** That part of the design rests on vendor guidance and inference.

**Reviews versus seller feedback.** Since 2023 Amazon has shown a "Customers say" paragraph labelled "AI-generated from the text of customer reviews". It is built only from Verified Purchase reviews and comes with aspect chips marked positive, negative or mixed ([About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/amazon-ai/amazon-ai-generated-review-highlights)). eBay keeps Detailed Seller Ratings (accuracy, shipping cost, shipping speed, communication) separate from product content ([eBay store example](https://ebay.com/str/outofthisworldelectronics/Cell-Phones-Accessories/_i.html)). Marketly should pool product reviews across all offers for the same product and keep seller ratings on the storefront and the other-offers list. Each review should note which seller fulfilled it.

**Coupons and dark patterns.** Amazon's clippable coupon is a small tag next to the price with a "Clip" control, and the discount is applied at checkout ([amzdudes, secondary](https://amzdudes.com/what-are-amazon-coupons-and-how-do-amazon-coupons-work/)). Its known weakness is eligibility tied to one variant or one seller, which causes "coupon not working" confusion when shoppers switch size ([thetoolstrunk](https://thetoolstrunk.com/amazon-clip-coupon-not-working/)). The bigger shift in 2025–2026 is regulatory. BEUC filed a complaint against Shein in June 2025 over pop-ups warning users they would lose promotions, countdown timers and notification spam (12 in one day on one phone) ([BNN Bloomberg, Jun 2025](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/company-news/2025/06/05/shein-hit-with-complaint-from-eu-consumer-group-over-dark-patterns/)). On 26 June 2026, Italy's AGCM fined a home-goods e-retailer **EUR 2M** for countdown timers that reset identically when they expired, stock indicators that reinforced perceived scarcity, and misleading promotional prices ([Baker McKenzie, Jun 2026](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/06/italy-agcm-eur-2m-fine-for-e-commerce-dark-patterns)). Marketly's flash sales should show only real countdowns that actually end the deal and only stock counts drawn from real inventory.

## Seven missing customer pages already have proven templates

Each undesigned destination page can be built from patterns that are already documented. The strength of the evidence varies a great deal from page to page, as summarised below. The phone versions should follow Marketly's existing rules (no footer and no breadcrumbs at phone width) and use bottom sheets for pickers.

| Page | Core pattern | Strongest evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full reviews page | Clickable star histogram, expanded by default; filtering by one star level at a time; sorting that weighs review count | Baymard ratings-distribution and sort studies | **Strong** (2024 update) |
| Product Q&A page | Search Q&A and reviews before asking; role-labelled answers; human Q&A stays visible beside any AI assistant | Baymard FAQ/Q&A study (2017) | **Medium** (old) |
| Delivery address picker | "Deliver to" pill → sheet with saved addresses, postcode entry and add-new; typo-tolerant autocomplete with manual fields visible | Baymard address lookup (2023), delivery-date research | **Strong** for entry, **weak** for the picker itself |
| Storefront "see all" and policies | Reuse the product-list component; separate Shipping, Returns and Contact sections with a "last updated" date | Baymard return-policy findings; Etsy and Amazon via secondary sources | **Medium** |
| Coupon eligible items | Coupon card with Clip/Clipped state; "X more to unlock" progress; a grid of eligible items | Amazon coverage in the press; practitioner copy guidance | **Weak** |
| Sign-in with backup code | "Try another way" → method list → single-use code field; prompt to add a passkey after recovery | NIST SP 800-63B-4 (Jul 2025), Google, GitHub, FIDO | **Strong** |
| Help article (customer and seller) | Title phrased as the question, summary first, numbered steps with deep links, related articles, escalation | NN/g (Dec 2020) | **Medium to weak** |

### Reviews and Q&A: the histogram is what shoppers use most

The full reviews page has the best evidence of any missing page. Baymard found that **95% of users relied on reviews**, and that the rating distribution was the most-used part of the reviews section, more than the review text. **90% of test participants tried to filter by clicking a rating bar**, and 53% actively looked for negative reviews. When the filter was collapsed by default, only 7% used it ([Baymard: ratings distribution, updated 2024](https://baymard.com/blog/user-ratings-distribution-summary)). Baymard gives five requirements: a graphical bar chart, clickable bars, expanded by default, filtering by one star level at a time rather than with checkboxes, and hiding the chart when there are five or fewer reviews. Sorting must take the number of reviews into account. 56% of surveyed shoppers chose a 4.5-star product with 12 ratings over a 5-star product with 2, yet 64% of sites sort by average alone ([Baymard: sort by ratings](https://baymard.com/research-articles/sort-by-customer-ratings)). Marketly's page should therefore open with average, count and a live histogram. Next comes an AI "Customers say" block labelled as generated from verified-purchase reviews, then filter chips (Verified purchase, With photos, Variant, keyword), then sort (Most helpful as default, Most recent, Highest, Lowest), then a swipeable photo strip, then review cards with Helpful and Report actions and replies labelled "Official Store" or "Seller". The AI summary is useful but does not replace the reviews. A University of Missouri study found shoppers use AI summaries as a starting point and distrust them when they can't tell how they were made ([MOspace](https://mospace.umsystem.edu/items/6ab57eb6-96fd-4f63-aff8-f2e7408e1ab2)). A 291-person experiment found that summaries alone did not change trust ([Católica thesis](https://ciencia.ucp.pt/en/studentTheses/lost-in-summarization/)). On phone, use "Load more" rather than infinite scroll. NN/g notes that infinite scroll makes items hard to find again and often sends Back to the top of the page ([NN/g, Sep 2022](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/infinite-scrolling-tips/)). No study compares the two specifically for review lists.

The Q&A page rests on older but clear evidence. 40% of test participants used community Q&A when it was available, and 25% read it with no specific question in mind. Shoppers find other customers' answers more credible than site copy. The common failures are unanswered questions, empty sections on new products and duplicate questions ([Baymard: FAQ and Q&A, Jul 2017](https://baymard.com/research-articles/product-page-faq-and-qa)). Marketly should place a "Search questions and reviews" box above "Ask a question" to cut duplicates. Answers should carry role badges (Seller, Official Store, Marketly staff, Verified buyer), and seller answers should not automatically outrank buyer answers. New questions should go to the seller and to recent buyers, with the expected answer time shown. Amazon's direction is the main caution here. A May 2026 forum post claims the browsable Q&A feed was replaced by Rufus and a keyword box. The thread is titled "Amazon continues to frustrate consumers looking for product info", but the poster sourced the claim from a Gemini chat, so it is **unverified** ([Motley Fool forum](https://discussion.fool.com/t/amazon-continues-to-frustrate-consumers-looking-for-product-info/124607)). An AI assistant can sit on top, labelled and citing the answers it used, but "See all questions" must stay one tap away.

### Address picker: tie the location to dates, stock and sellers

Address entry fails often enough to justify careful design. **9% of manually typed shipping addresses contained typos**, 55% of sites lack fully automatic address lookup, and 69% of participants fell back to typing when suggestions failed. Autocomplete must tolerate misspellings, rank suggestions by proximity rather than alphabetically, and always show the manual fields as a fallback ([Baymard: address lookup, Mar 2023](https://baymard.com/blog/automatic-address-lookup)). The picker's purpose is to show delivery facts: users prefer concrete dates to shipping speeds, yet 41% of benchmarked sites give no date ([Baymard: speed vs date](https://baymard.com/blog/shipping-speed-vs-delivery-date)). Baymard's 2025 checkout article puts the figure at 48%, so the exact share depends on the benchmark year. Amazon's "Choose your location" lets shoppers enter a postcode or pick a saved address, and the sellers, prices and shipping rates shown change with the location ([Repricer support, secondary](https://support.repricer.com/troubleshooting-changing-location-see-all-competitors-amazon)). Marketly's picker should be a "Deliver to [suburb/postcode]" pill in the phone top bar. It opens a sheet with saved addresses as radio cards (default first), "Use my current location" (which asks for location permission only when tapped), postcode entry with Apply, and "Add new address". After the shopper applies a location, update delivery dates, shipping cost, stock and the sellers who can deliver in place, and flag items that can't ship there. **No primary documentation of any major marketplace's picker was retrieved**, and no research was found on how shoppers react when a location change alters prices.

### Storefront, policies and coupons: put platform rules beside seller rules

For storefronts and policies, the key evidence concerns where shoppers look. 60% look for the return policy on the product page, and users routinely check the footer for shipping and returns information ([Baymard Product Page 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux); [Baymard: footer links](https://baymard.com/blog/footer-needs-return-shipping-links)). Marketly's phone layout has no footer, so the product page's seller line and the storefront's Policies tab have to carry this information. Etsy splits shop policies into returns and exchanges, cancellations and privacy, shows a last-updated date and sets returns per listing ([Craftybase, secondary](https://app.craftybase.com/blog/how-to-set-up-etsy-shop-policies)). Amazon went the other way. Since 1 August 2022, seller profiles point to Amazon's own return policy, and sellers can no longer post custom ones ([SellerEngine](https://sellerengine.com/mastering-returns-and-refunds-a-guide-to-amazons-latest-guidance/amp/)). The hybrid lesson is to show the **Marketly guarantee alongside each seller's policy**, so buyers know which rules apply regardless of seller. The storefront header should carry the logo, identity badge, rating with count, time on Marketly, response time, Follow and Contact. The tabs should be Home, All items, Reviews, Policies and About. "All items" should reuse the standard product list, with search inside the store, categories, filters and an item count. A Report seller link belongs in the storefront too ([Rigby, Jun 2025](https://www.rigbyjs.com/blog/marketplace-ux)).

The coupon eligible-items page has the weakest evidence of the set: **no Baymard or NN/g research covers it**. Amazon's coupon hub offers search, categories, a "Your coupons" list and sorting by savings, expiry or popularity, and the discount is applied at checkout rather than always in the cart ([Yahoo Lifestyle](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/amazon-secret-coupons-091514031.html)). Practitioner guidance says to state the amount still needed ("You're X away") rather than the rule ([ninety9.dev](https://ninety9.dev/blog/free-shipping-bar-copy.html)). Marketly's page should have a coupon card showing discount, minimum spend, who funds it (Official Store, a seller or Marketly), expiry, whether it stacks with other offers, and a Clip button that changes to "Clipped, applies at checkout". Below it should be a sticky progress bar computed from eligible items already in the cart, and a grid with quick add and "in cart" markers. Unlike Amazon, Marketly should show the discount line in the cart as well, and say inline when a variant switch makes an item ineligible.

### Backup-code sign-in and help articles: follow the standard, then the task

Backup-code sign-in has the most authoritative evidence, because it follows a standard. NIST SP 800-63B-4 (final, July 2025) requires recovery codes to be **usable only once**. It requires at least one phishing-resistant option at AAL2, treats SMS as "restricted" with alternatives that must be offered, requires sites to allow password managers and autofill, and says sites should allow paste ([NIST SP 800-63B-4](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html)). Google's 2-Step Verification screen offers "Try another way", then "Enter one of your 8-digit backup codes". It states that Google never asks for a backup code anywhere except at sign-in ([Google Account Help](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/1187538?hl=en)). GitHub lists app, security key, passkey, SMS and mobile push, with recovery codes as the fallback ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/authentication/securing-your-account-with-two-factor-authentication-2fa/accessing-github-using-two-factor-authentication)). Google's passkey guidance adds a useful step: **prompt users to create a passkey right after they recover their account**, because security is on their mind at that moment ([Google for Developers](https://developers.google.com/identity/passkeys/ux/user-journeys)). Marketly's screen should be one field that ignores spaces and hyphens and accepts paste, with copy explaining what a recovery code is. On success it should say "This code is now used; N left", send a security email and offer to regenerate codes and add a passkey. A "Lost access to everything?" link should lead to a slower recovery through support, not an instant bypass. The FIDO Alliance points out that a phishable fallback undermines a phishing-resistant account ([FIDO Design Guidelines](https://fidoalliance.org/design-guidelines)). Sellers control payouts, so Marketly should require passkeys for sellers and for high-value actions. No usability study with success or drop-off rates for recovery-code entry was found.

Help articles have the thinnest research base, and most of it predates 2025. NN/g's guidance (December 2020) is to make search strong, open with a short summary, give concrete numbered steps instead of long overviews, and prefer help the user pulls up during a task over help pushed at them ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/help-and-documentation/)). A customer or seller article in Marketly should have the title phrased as the user's question, a one-line answer, an "Applies to: Buyers/Sellers" tag, a last-updated date, numbered steps with deep links into the right app screen, three to five related articles, "Was this helpful?" and escalation to contact after the content. Common features such as helpfulness votes and escalation placement are widespread but **not supported by any research found**.

## Back-office screens should copy Stripe, Shopify and GitHub conventions

Seller tools have their own reputation ranking. Stripe Dashboard and Shopify Admin are the usual positive references. Amazon Seller Central, Etsy Shop Manager and eBay Seller Hub draw most of the complaints. Seller complaints are almost all about **lost efficiency, not looks**. Sellers complain when setting stock to zero takes more clicks than it used to ([Amazon Seller Forums](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/307e6c16-395a-4061-843c-ca3d7e75b940)), about redesigns described as "only cosmetic with no added functionality" ([EcommerceBytes, older](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/?p=3433)), about support cases "tossed between internal teams" in 2025 ([Amazon Seller Forums](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/851515e5-12a6-42e0-896c-4d7fdb79dedf)), and about AI features such as auto-generated alt text switched on by default ([Value Added Resource, Apr 2025](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/etsy-shop-manager-updates-spring-2025/)). Sellers work on six marketplaces on average ([ChannelEngine 2025](https://www.channelengine.com/en/blog/marketplace-seller-trends-report-2025?hsLang=en)), so they bring Amazon and Shopify habits with them. Marketly should follow familiar conventions (list → detail → save; filters above tables; inline price and stock edits on list rows) and treat any extra click on a frequent task as a regression. Praise for Stripe comes from design blogs, not merchant surveys ([925 Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/stripe-dashboard-design-breakdown)), and **no reliable 2025–2026 UX evidence exists for Walmart Seller Center, Zalando zDirect or Mercado Libre's seller tools**.

**Create and edit forms.** The design systems mostly agree. IBM Carbon recommends single-column layouts with short labels above each field, marking whichever group is smaller ("(optional)" if most fields are required), visible helper text rather than tooltips, specific errors shown when a field loses focus, and button labels that name the task ("Create role", never "Submit"). Carbon also sets the container rule. Use a **dedicated page** for complex or multi-step forms, a **dialog** for rare, critical edits with fewer than five inputs, and a **side panel** for repeated input that needs the surrounding context ([Carbon Forms](https://carbondesignsystem.com/patterns/forms-pattern/)). Applied to Marketly: **Add product** gets a dedicated page with sections and a contextual save bar, which Shopify shows only when there are unsaved changes, with Save and Discard ([Polaris](https://polaris.shopify.com/components/deprecated/contextual-save-bar)). **Add role** gets a dedicated page because of its permission matrix. **New case** opens as a side panel when started from an order or listing, so the context stays visible, and as a page when started from the cases list. The systems disagree on two points, and Marketly should write down a rule for each. On disabled buttons, Primer says never disable Save, because disabled buttons can't receive focus and have low contrast, while Carbon allows disabling until valid on short forms ([Primer Saving](https://primer.style/product/ui-patterns/saving/)). On toasts, Shopify uses them to confirm bulk actions, while Primer avoids them for accessibility reasons ([Shopify index table](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/app-home/patterns/compositions/index-table)). A workable rule is never to disable Save on long forms, and to use toasts only for background actions that also leave a lasting record ("Export started" plus an Exports list), with inline messages for save results and errors.

**Audit log entry detail.** Good audit logs are structured event streams. GitHub entries record actor, action, affected user, repository, country and time. They support search qualifiers such as `actor:`, `action:` and `created:2026-07-01..2026-07-31`, export to JSON or CSV within 100 MB or 10 minutes, and keep 180 days of history ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/organizations/keeping-your-organization-secure/managing-security-settings-for-your-organization/reviewing-the-audit-log-for-your-organization)). Google Workspace defaults to the last 7 days ([Workspace Help](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/reports/run-a-search-in-the-audit-and-investigation-tool?hl=en)). Since April 2026 it adds resource owner details and the actor's device information to events ([Workspace Updates, Apr 2026](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/04/workspace-audit-logs-new-functionality-and-expanded-event-fields-in-the-admin-console.html)). Stripe Workbench shows the list on the left and the detail on the right, with the raw payload, a map of related objects and a Copy link button ([Stripe Workbench](https://docs.stripe.com/workbench/overview)). Marketly's entry page should lead with a readable sentence ("Jane Doe (Support Agent) changed payout hold on Seller ACME") and show the machine action and an outcome badge beside it. A fact panel should list the actor and their role at the time, the target and its owner, the time in local time and UTC, the origin (IP, device, and which app: Seller Center, Admin or API), and the request ID and token ID with copy buttons. That last field fits Marketly's rule that every endpoint needs a token. Below that should come a diff showing only changed fields, with raw JSON behind a disclosure, then related events sharing the same request or target, then "filter the log by this actor/target" chips. There should be no edit or delete, because the log is append-only. **None of the cited products document a before/after diff UI**; that recommendation is inferred.

**Trust and risk report detail.** Stripe Radar and Shopify's fraud analysis give the clearest template. Show a banded verdict first. Then show a *few* factors, each marked as raising or lowering risk. Shopify separates fraud indicators, legitimate indicators and neutral details, and warns that "individual indicators… don't represent the order's overall risk level" ([Shopify Help](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/managing-orders/protecting-orders/fraud-analysis)). Stripe adds "Show all insights", a related-payments view that links shared cards, IPs and customers, and "Assign to me" with filters for mine and unassigned. Its reviewers use J/K keys to move to the next or previous case without returning to the list. Every action states its consequence ("Refund… permanent, you can't undo it"), and a timeline records every assignment and action ([Stripe Radar reviews](https://docs.stripe.com/radar/reviews); [Risk insights](https://docs.stripe.com/radar/reviews/risk-insights)). Stripe's Smart Refunds even shows the probability attached to each confidence level, from 72% for "very high" down to 15%. Marketly's page should have a header with case ID, status, SLA timer, assignee and queue navigation; a verdict card (risk band first, score second, one sentence on why the case was flagged); three to five grouped factors; evidence tabs for related entities, attachments and the seller's history; a recommended next step in plain words; a fixed set of actions (Clear, Request info, Restrict listing, Hold payouts, Suspend), each stating whether it can be reversed and requiring a reason code that goes to the audit log; and a full timeline. The seller-facing version is Amazon's Account Health: one score from 0 to 1,000 with bands (below 100 unhealthy, 100–199 at risk, 200+ healthy), three category cards and a violation list graded by severity ([Seller Assistant, Jun 2025](https://www.sellerassistant.app/blog/what-is-amazon-seller-account-health-rating/)). Those figures come from third-party explainers, because Amazon's own pages require a login. **How marketplace operators build their internal trust-and-safety consoles is undocumented**, since those tools are proprietary.

**Report detail and export flows.** Stripe reports default to the previous month, treat chosen dates as inclusive, show the time zone, and publish data freshness on the page (balance data within 12 hours). They also offer scheduled daily, weekly or monthly delivery with an email when the report is ready ([Stripe Report options](https://docs.stripe.com/reports/options)). Shopify's product export asks for scope (current page, all, selected, or current filter results) and format, then **decides the delivery method automatically**: a browser download for small exports and email for large ones, which also notifies the store owner when a staff member exports ([Shopify Help](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/import-export/export-products)). A common merchant confusion is exported numbers that don't match on-screen analytics because metric definitions differ ([Highview Apps](https://www.highviewapps.com/kb/why-are-the-values-from-the-export-dont-match-shopify-analytics-reports/)). That is an argument for putting a definitions panel next to each metric and repeating the definitions in the export header. Marketly's report page should offer a date preset, comparison to the previous period or the same period last year, a visible time zone, a seller-scope picker for admins, KPI deltas above a chart and a table showing the same numbers, and a line such as "Data updated 2 h ago". The export dialog should ask for scope, format and columns. Small exports download at once. Large ones run in the background with a toast, appear in an Exports list (queued → ready → expired) and trigger a notification. Admin exports of seller data should write an audit entry. Export link expiry and row limits are **inferred**; neither Stripe nor Shopify documents them.

**Seller help article.** The seller help article should follow the customer article template with two changes. "Still need help? Open a case" should **prefill the New case form** with the article and category, which addresses the "case goes nowhere" complaint. The same content should also open in a side help panel from "Learn more" links, so sellers keep any unsaved form data. Carbon's empty-state pattern supports linking to docs as a secondary action ([Carbon Empty states](https://carbondesignsystem.com/patterns/empty-states-pattern/)). Stripe's Errors tab, which groups errors by type with "learn more about how to resolve each type", shows how to embed help in the work itself ([Stripe Workbench](https://docs.stripe.com/workbench/overview)). Beyond these, the guidance on help articles and onboarding checklists comes from practitioner blogs ([Calabro](https://trevorcalabro.substack.com/p/onboarding-and-in-app-help-strategy)), not research.

## Evidence is strong for basics, thin outside the US and Europe

The evidence base is uneven, and Marketly's design decisions should reflect that. The strongest material is Baymard's 2025–2026 benchmarks: independent, recent, large-sample and specific. Its limits are that per-site scores sit behind a paywall, it does not cover Asian or Latin American marketplaces, and several underlying studies (Q&A 2017, address lookup 2023, ratings distribution 2024) predate the window. Official documentation from Stripe, Shopify, GitHub, Google and NIST is authoritative for the back office and authentication, but it describes how those products work, not whether users succeed with them. Most marketplace-specific patterns rest on partner documentation or secondary explainers.

| Claim area | Evidence quality | Main caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Fundamentals: checkout, product page, search, mobile, navigation failure rates | **Strong**: Baymard 2025–2026 | Per-site ranks paywalled |
| Who leads on UX (Baymard awards) | **Strong** | Awards by industry/theme, not an overall ranking |
| Satisfaction ranks (ACSI 2025, Forrester 2025) | **Strong** | ACSI 2026 figures from a secondary source, primary returned 403 |
| Reviews histogram, rating-aware sorting | **Strong** | 2024 update; no data on photo or variant filters |
| Recovery codes, passkeys, 2FA alternatives | **Strong**: NIST, Google, GitHub | No usability metrics for code entry |
| Back-office forms, tables, audit, risk, export | **Strong for patterns**: official docs | Diff UI and export expiry inferred |
| Seller disclosure, badges, condition grades | **Medium**: Zalando/Mercado Libre docs, Marketplace Pulse | Amazon and Walmart layouts from secondary sources |
| Multi-seller cart grouping | **Weak**: marketplace-software vendors | No observed teardown of Amazon, Walmart or Shopee carts |
| Coupons, eligible-items pages | **Weak**: press and secondary blogs | No research at all |
| Amazon Q&A replaced by Rufus | **Very weak**: one forum post citing a Gemini chat | Undocumented by Amazon |
| Sticky add-to-cart lift, Core Web Vitals revenue claims | **Weak**: vendors and aggregators | Methods unclear or data old |
| Shopee, Mercado Libre, Rakuten, Target Plus UX quality | **Missing** | No independent UX score; Target Plus only via a low-quality SEO page |
| IKEA, Allbirds, Glossier, Warby Parker, Patagonia, Sephora rankings | **Missing** | Reputation rests on listicles; scattered Baymard credits only |
| Address picker UI at major marketplaces, help-article layout | **Missing** | No primary teardowns retrieved |

Two gaps matter most for Marketly. First, nobody has published research on how shoppers read seller identity versus fulfilment in a hybrid store. The two-axis badge model and the "Sold by / Ships from" line are sound inferences from Walmart, Zalando and Shopee, not tested findings. Marketly should put them through its own usability testing before launch. Second, the lack of independent evidence for Shopee and Mercado Libre means their feature-heavy, deal-driven interfaces cannot be shown to be good UX. The ACSI result for Temu, which uses a similar playbook, points the other way.

## Conclusion

Marketly's advantage will not come from looking like Amazon or Apple. It will come from being one of the rare stores that applies the guidelines most leading sites skip, and then adding a layer that single-brand stores never need: making each seller's identity, performance, delivery promise and return rules as clear as the product itself. The hybrid model is what makes this both harder and more valuable. The Official Store can offer brand-store simplicity: about 10 categories, a platform-backed returns policy and real delivery dates. Third-party offers inherit the same frame through standard seller lines, earned badges and per-seller cart groups. Nordstrom, the closest hybrid example with top independent scores, shows the two can coexist.

The undesigned pages are less of a design problem than they look. Five of them (reviews, sign-in recovery, audit entry, trust report, create forms) can be built from strong, documented templates, so Marketly should commit to those designs now. The remaining pages (coupon eligible items, storefront "see all", address picker, help articles, multi-seller cart wording) have no external evidence to settle them. For those, Marketly's own usability testing will be worth more than further desk research. With the EU now fining fake urgency, the restrained design that reads as "simple" is also the legally safe one.
