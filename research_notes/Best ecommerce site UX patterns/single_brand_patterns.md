# What makes the best single-brand / DTC stores feel simple and smart (2025–2026)

Research date: 2026-10-12. Main independent source: Baymard Institute's 2025–2026 benchmark articles (free summaries). Most per-brand scores (Warby Parker, Glossier, Apple case studies) are behind Baymard's paywall, so brand-specific claims below are thinner than general pattern claims. Unless marked otherwise, statistics are from Baymard's large-scale benchmarks of leading US/EU sites, not from DTC brands alone.

Source quality: Baymard counts as primary, independent research. UserTesting case studies are vendor marketing, so they are secondary but give concrete numbers. Agency blogs and stats aggregators are labelled as weaker wherever they are used.

---

## 1. Navigation and information architecture

### Takeaway
Simple navigation comes from a shallow, well-split category tree: about 10 subcategories per level, clickable parent headers, the current section highlighted, and menus that open after a short hover delay. Category landing pages should put subcategory navigation (with thumbnails) above promotions. Most big sites still get these basics wrong, so doing them well stands out.

### Cited Findings
- Baymard's 2025 homepage and category navigation benchmark: 58% of desktop sites and 67% of mobile sites perform "mediocre" to "poor". On desktop, no site scored "good" or better. The benchmark covers 16,000+ scores from 180+ US/EU sites. — [Baymard, Homepage & Category Navigation UX 2025](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice)
- **Split categories at about 10 options.** 60% of sites don't. Too many subcategories overwhelm users, and on mobile the long scroll weakens information scent. Baymard also recommends 10+ products at the deepest level. Ashley Furniture's huge "Furniture" menu is the negative example ("whoa, there's a lot of options here!"). — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice)
- **Highlight the current scope in the main nav.** 95% of sites don't. Marks & Spencer is praised for highlighting the active top-level category in green. Keurig's mobile nav is criticised for giving no sense of location. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice)
- **Make dropdown headers clickable.** 33% of sites don't. **Allbirds is praised by name**: clicking "Shoes" in the dropdown shows all shoes before the shopper picks a subcategory. Argos's static "Headphones & Audio" header is the counter-example. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice)
- **Add a hover delay to dropdowns.** 61% of sites don't. Baymard recommends a 300–500 ms delay plus mouse-path logic so neighbouring menus don't open by accident. Macy's is praised. H&M's large dropdown is criticised because it triggers too easily. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice)
- **Category landing pages: subcategory navigation first.** 76% of sites get the hierarchy wrong. **Nike's "Men" category page is criticised by name**: a prominent featured link overshadowed the text-only subcategory links. Argos buried subcategories under offers. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice)
- **Subcategory thumbnails on landing pages.** 55% of sites don't use them. They should be small "visual labels". Wayfair and Smyths are praised. Amazon's text-only electronics links and Zalando are criticised. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice)
- **Keep the homepage free of aggressive ads.** 55% of sites fall short. Avoid ads in prime positions and avoid pop-up overlays. LEGO is praised. GAP's "My Offers" overlays and Home Depot's ad-heavy app homepage are criticised. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice)
- **Carousels.** Desktop slides should rotate after 5–7 s (10 s if text-heavy) and pause on hover. Mobile carousels should not auto-rotate. Static stacked sections, as Williams Sonoma uses, are a valid alternative. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice)
- **Clear scope labels on homepage links.** 59% of mobile sites don't have them. Write "Women's New Arrivals", not just "New Arrivals". Banana Republic's "50% off" tile misled users about scope. Columbia's "Shop Sale" button, which opens a scope chooser, is praised. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice)
- **Shoppable lifestyle images.** 70% of sites don't link them to the products shown. West Elm's mobile tappable tags (with product count and links) and Argos are praised. Dick's Sporting Goods is criticised. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice)
- **Clear hit areas.** 51% of sites have ambiguous ones. On mobile, each visual element should have one hit area. Wayfair and HP are praised. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice)
- Gymshark organises products by activity and fit. This comes from an agency/inspiration blog, so it is weak evidence. — [UserTesting search snippet / blackbird.it.com case listing](https://www.blackbird.it.com/cases/gym-shark-case-study)
- Apple redesigned its online store and brought back a dedicated "Store" tab (removed in 2015). The new store uses horizontally scrolling cards that match the iOS Store app, and gives each product line (Mac, iPhone, AirPods…) its own Store page listing the available models. Reactions were mixed: welcome for discovery, but some said "too much going on" and the left padding looked lopsided on desktop. The cards are described as "mobile-first" and work better on phones than on desktop. I could not fetch the full article text to confirm the exact date (I believe it is around 2022). — [Creative Bloq (search snippet)](https://creativebloq.com/news/apple-store-redesign)

### Inferences
- The DTC "simple menu" feel is mostly a result of small catalogues (Allbirds, Glossier, Warby Parker). A marketplace Official Store can recreate it by limiting each brand store to about 10 top categories, making headers clickable, and highlighting the active one, rather than inheriting the marketplace's full taxonomy.
- Baymard's Nike example warns that brand-heavy editorial landing pages hurt findability when promotions sit above subcategories. Official Store landing pages should show subcategory tiles first and campaigns second.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025–2026 NN/g article fetched specifically on mega menus versus simple menus. NN/g's older "mega menus work well" guidance (c. 2009/2017) is well known but was not verified in this pass.
- No public source documents IKEA's or Patagonia's 2025–2026 navigation structure.

## 2. Product pages

### Takeaway
The PDP patterns that independent research rewards most are concrete:
- size and variant buttons instead of dropdowns
- images on a model or in scale
- a visible size guide or fit help
- shipping cost and return policy shown on the PDP
- delivery shown as a date
- reviews with photo browsing and brand replies to negative reviews

Virtual try-on (Warby Parker) and prominent fit guides (Gymshark) are the standout "smart" brand features.

### Cited Findings
- Baymard's 2026 PDP benchmark: only 48% of desktop sites, 38% of mobile sites and 36% of apps reach "decent" or better. No site is "perfect". It covers 30,000+ scores from 155+ sites, and the article was updated 2026-03-18. — [Baymard, Product Page UX 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- **Size buttons, not dropdowns.** 57% of sites don't use them. Buttons show every size and make out-of-stock sizes visible straight away. Marks & Spencer is praised. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- **In-scale images.** 42% of users try to judge size from images, and 37% of sites give no scale reference. GoPro is praised. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- **Images on a human model for wearables, accessories and cosmetics.** 23% of sites don't have them. Madewell is criticised. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- **Shipping cost estimate on the PDP.** 67% of sites don't show one. Show the lowest shipping cost and offer an expandable tax/fee calculator. Ann Taylor is criticised. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- **Return policy on the PDP.** 60% of users look for return information on the PDP, and 15% have abandoned an order over an unsatisfactory return policy. 44% of sites don't link it. Boohoo is praised for showing delivery and returns information. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- **Guest wishlist.** 89% of sites implement saving poorly, often by forcing account creation. **Adidas is criticised by name** for forcing an account to save items. BonPrix is praised for its guest wishlist. 21% of shoppers rely on save features. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- **Reviews.** 89% of sites don't reply to negative reviews. Replies should be visually marked as staff. Cyberport is praised. 63% of sites don't let users swipe across all reviewer photos. Lamps Plus is praised and HP is criticised. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- **Gift options shown at product level.** 78% of sites don't. Etsy is praised. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- **Unit price for multi-quantity items.** 81% of sites don't show it. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- **Delivery as a date, not a speed.** 48% of sites show "2–4 business days". **Apple is praised by name** for showing an actual arrival date. Baymard also recommends a cut-off countdown ("Order in next 43 minutes for Tuesday delivery"), which 83% of sites don't show; Best Buy is praised. — [Baymard, Checkout UX 2025](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-UX)
- **Gymshark fit guidance.** After A/B testing, Gymshark made its size guide more prominent, and size-guide use rose 40% within six weeks. Testing also showed shoppers left the funnel to check Google and YouTube reviews, so Gymshark made it easier to keep items in the cart while browsing elsewhere. Checkout rates rose 11%. This is a vendor case study and is undated. — [UserTesting customer story: Gymshark](https://www.usertesting.com/resources/customers/gymshark)
- **Warby Parker virtual try-on** (older evidence, launched 2019):
  - It runs in the iOS app on ARKit with the Face ID / TrueDepth camera.
  - A proprietary algorithm places the frames on the face.
  - 3D artists rendered each frame photorealistically.
  - Shoppers can take a screenshot to share for a second opinion.
  - It replaced an earlier web try-on tool that had been removed for technical reasons.
  
  — [Retail Dive, 2019](https://www.retaildive.com/news/warby-parker-launches-ar-tool-for-virtual-try-on/547673); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1778199)
- Baymard first benchmarked Warby Parker in July 2025: 22 desktop and 24 mobile pages. Publicly visible marker counts are desktop PDP 21 positive / 13 negative and mobile PDP 24 positive / 13 negative. Baymard's public teaser says eyewear sites "often support early visual browsing well but break down when users need stronger decision support around fit, refinement, checkout recovery…". Scores are paywalled. — [Baymard case study: Warby Parker](https://baymard.com/ux-benchmark/case-studies/warby-parker)
- Baymard first benchmarked Glossier in August 2026 (19 desktop and 19 mobile pages; desktop homepage 9 positive / 4 negative markers). Scores are paywalled. — [Baymard case study: Glossier](https://baymard.com/ux-benchmark/case-studies/glossier)
- **Apple comparison.** A design-press piece says Apple's product comparisons sit "about halfway down the page but leave much to be desired". UX Collective published "It's not just you: shopping at Apple is really complicated", but I could not fetch it (403), so its specific arguments are unverified. — [Creative Bloq snippet](https://creativebloq.com/news/apple-store-redesign); [UX Collective (title only)](https://uxdesign.cc/its-not-just-you-shopping-at-apple-is-really-complicated-ce1f8d1c29af)
- Baymard's annotated Apple product page (2023-10-06) has "45 highlights" of right and wrong practice, but the details are paywalled. — [Baymard example](https://baymard.com/ecommerce-design-examples/41-product-page/16682-apple)

### Inferences
- An Official Store PDP should show these in one compact block near the buy button: size or colour buttons with out-of-stock states, an "Arrives Thu 15 Oct" estimate with a cut-off countdown, shipping cost, and a one-line returns summary linking to the full policy. That covers several of Baymard's most-missed items at once.
- A guest wishlist (saved per device and merged at login) is a cheap win, since 89% of sites miss it.
- Fit guidance doesn't need AR to work. Gymshark's gain came from making an existing size guide more prominent. A marketplace can require brands to provide size charts and show the link next to the size buttons.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025–2026 independent source on Allbirds' own size/fit tooling (for example, a "fits true to size, if between sizes size up" note), sticky add-to-bag on these brands, or Apple's configurator. These are commonly observed on the live sites but were not sourced here.
- No source gave the current status of Warby Parker's web-based virtual try-on, or Home Try-On in 2025–2026.
- Sephora's PDP features (shade finder, Virtual Artist) were not sourced in this pass.

## 3. Search

### Takeaway
Search that feels smart has three parts:
- autocomplete that tolerates typos
- results that switch product thumbnails to the colour or variant searched for
- queries that persist, plus sensible scoping

Most sites fail these, so solid fundamentals beat flashy features.

### Cited Findings
- Baymard's 2026 search benchmark: 56% of sites do not adequately support shoppers' search needs ("mediocre or worse"). — [Baymard, Ecommerce Search UX](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-search-report-and-benchmark)
- 80% of sites offer autocomplete, but only 19% implement it well. 58% don't copy the active (keyboard-highlighted) suggestion into the search field. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-search-report-and-benchmark)
- 69% don't give autocomplete suggestions for misspelled queries. Baymard warns that if a minor typo produces odd suggestions, users conclude the store doesn't carry the item, switch strategy, or leave. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-search-report-and-benchmark)
- 54% fail to update product thumbnails to match the searched variant (for example, "red dress" should show the red variant's image). — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-search-report-and-benchmark)
- 46% get auto-directing to a matching category scope wrong. 37% don't keep the search query across sessions. 21% of mobile sites lack a search submit button. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-search-report-and-benchmark)
- To avoid empty "no results" pages, the recommendations are typo tolerance and search-as-you-type that suggests products, collections, pages and queries before the shopper submits. This is a secondary source summarising Baymard. — [Shopify blog, "No results found"](https://www.shopify.com/au/blog/no-results-found)

### Inferences
- The minimum for an Official Store search: within-store scope by default with a one-tap "search all of the marketplace" fallback, typo-tolerant autocomplete that shows product thumbnails and categories, variant-matched thumbnails, and a no-results page that offers corrected spellings, related categories and popular items instead of a dead end.

### Gaps
- I found no brand-specific 2025–2026 evaluation of Apple, Nike, Sephora or IKEA search. Baymard's named examples for search are paywalled.

## 4. Cart and checkout

### Takeaway
The best checkouts are short and forgiving:
- guest checkout as the most prominent option
- about 7–8 real input fields
- optional fields collapsed and both required and optional fields marked
- specific error messages
- delivery shown as dates
- all fulfilment options on one step
- +/- quantity steppers

Express wallets (Apple Pay, Shop Pay) sit at the top. Even so, almost no site is rated "good".

### Cited Findings
- Baymard's checkout benchmark (updated 2025-11-25): 64% of desktop sites, 63% of mobile sites and 46% of apps have mediocre or worse checkout UX. Only 2% of desktop/mobile sites and 7% of apps rate "good", and none are "perfect". Large sites can gain up to 35% conversion through checkout design changes. — [Baymard, Checkout UX 2025](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-UX)
- About 70% of users abandon after adding to cart, and 18% abandoned because they were forced to create an account. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-UX)
- **Guest checkout should be the most prominent option** on the account step. 62% of sites don't do this. Etsy is praised and Room & Board is criticised. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-UX)
- **Password rules.** 65% of sites are too complex; a 6–8 character minimum is enough. A poor password-reset flow causes 19% abandonment among returning users. Dollar Shave Club is praised. Target and Cabela's are criticised. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-UX)
- **Quantity +/- buttons.** 97% of sites don't optimise this; text fields lead to "1" becoming "21". Walmart is praised. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-UX)
- **Show all fulfilment options together** (shipping, local delivery, store pickup). 52% of sites don't. **IKEA UK is praised by name.** Madewell and Target are criticised. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-UX)
- **Mark both required and optional fields.** 61% of sites don't. 32% of participants skipped required fields when marking was unclear. Walmart is praised. Everlane and Wayfair are criticised. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-UX)
- **Hide rarely used optional fields** (such as Address line 2) behind a link, as Lowe's does. 32% of sites choose the wrong interface. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-UX)
- **Specific error messages.** 94% of sites are too generic; "Phone number incomplete" is better than "Invalid phone". Away (a DTC luggage brand) is praised. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-UX)
- **Explain why the phone number is needed.** 49% of sites don't, and over 70% of respondents are reluctant to give one. **Apple is criticised by name** for not explaining. American Eagle is praised. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-UX)
- An ideal checkout has 12–14 form elements (about 7–8 inputs), against a US average of 23.48 by default. The average abandonment rate is 70.19% (2025). Abandonment reasons: extra costs 39%, slow delivery 21%, distrust with card details 19%, forced account 19%. These figures are attributed to Baymard via secondary summaries; the primary page was not fetched in this pass. — [Baymard, Checkout usability (via search snippet)](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-checkout-usability); [Social Intents summary](https://www.socialintents.com/blog/?p=3951)

### Inferences
- These Baymard findings translate directly to an Official Store and marketplace checkout:
  - guest-first entry
  - express wallet buttons above the form
  - address autocomplete with Address line 2 collapsed
  - every fulfilment option on one step
  - delivery dates per shipment
  - +/- steppers in the cart
  - inline specific errors
  - an explained phone field
- For multi-seller carts, splitting delivery dates per seller or shipment keeps the "date, not speed" rule.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025–2026 independent data on the conversion lift from Shop Pay or Apple Pay specifically on these brands (Shopify's own claims were not fetched and would be vendor claims).
- I found no source on progress-indicator design for these specific brands.

## 5. Account, order tracking and returns self-service

### Takeaway
The evidence is thin. Self-service return portals that need only an order number and email are standard (Patagonia), but even praised brands have gaps, such as Patagonia's returns not being trackable. Baymard's data says accounts should never block buying or saving.

### Cited Findings
- Patagonia's self-service return flow (EU): enter order number and email in the return portal, select items and a reason code, then print a label and form. Customers **cannot track their return** and are told to keep the shipping receipt. — [Patagonia EU returns](https://eu.patagonia.com/dk/en/returns.html)
- Forcing an account to save items is a named anti-pattern (Adidas). — [Baymard PDP](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- Forced account creation causes 18–19% of abandonments. Poor password reset causes 19% abandonment among existing users. — [Baymard Checkout](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-UX)
- The Nike app markets "easy shipping and returns" as a Member benefit, alongside Member Rewards and personalised style advice. — [Nike app listing via AppPricingLab](https://apppricinglab.com/app/apple/1095459556)
- The IKEA app includes order tracking, stock checking, saved lists and secure payments. — [IKEA app page](https://www.ikea.com/us/en/ikea-app/)

### Inferences
- Return status tracking is a place where a marketplace can beat a respected DTC brand (Patagonia).
- Guest order lookup by order number and email should exist alongside the logged-in account.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025–2026 independent evaluation (Baymard's account/self-service benchmark is paywalled) of Nike, Sephora, Apple or Warby Parker order tracking or return flows.

## 6. Mobile apps and mobile web

### Takeaway
The top brand apps rate about 4.8–4.9 stars and earn their place through loyalty and membership (Nike, Sephora) and practical utility (IKEA: in-store navigation, stock checks, room scanning, planners), not by copying the website. Independent benchmarks find apps and mobile web often worse than desktop, so being mobile-first doesn't guarantee quality.

### Cited Findings
- The IKEA app is rated **4.8/5 on the App Store (as of April 2026)**. Features include:
  - personalised offers and recommendations
  - saved lists
  - in-store navigation
  - interactive kitchen planners
  - room scanning to visualise products
  - secure payments and order tracking
  - stock checking
  - IKEA Live expert content
  
  — [AppFollow IKEA listing](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/ikea/1452164827?country=us); [IKEA app page](https://www.ikea.com/us/en/ikea-app/)
- The Nike app is reported at 4.9 stars, with a minimalist black/white/grey look. This is a weaker secondary source. — [screensdesign / search snippet](https://screensdesign.com/apps/ikea/)
- The Sephora app holds 4.9 stars (Singapore App Store listing, 5.3K ratings; the Brazil app has 4.9 from 41.2K). — [Apple App Store SG: Sephora](https://apps.apple.com/sg/app/sephora-beauty-shopping/id1152899196); [AppPricingLab](https://apppricinglab.com/app/apple/1580559166)
- Baymard finds apps perform worst on product pages (only 36% decent or better, against 48% on desktop) and mobile web at 38%. Mobile navigation is mediocre or worse on 67% of sites. — [Baymard PDP](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux); [Baymard Nav](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice)
- On checkout, apps do comparatively better: 46% are mediocre or worse, against 63–64% for web, and 7% rate "good" against 2%. — [Baymard Checkout](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-UX)
- In Gymshark's app, fixing a workout-plan creation error doubled app users within a month (vendor case study). — [UserTesting: Gymshark](https://www.usertesting.com/resources/customers/gymshark)
- A 2023 student design critique of the IKEA iOS app exists (Pratt), but it is older and academic. — [Pratt IXD critique, 2023](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2023/09/design-critique-ikea-ios-app/)

### Inferences
- These apps succeed through utility tied to the brand relationship: membership perks, store mode, planners, AR. For a marketplace, Official Store value inside the native app would come from follow-the-brand feeds, member offers, stock or pickup checks and saved lists, not from duplicating the web PDP.
- Mobile carousels should not auto-rotate (Baymard), and each card should have one hit area. Both matter directly for a phone-first layout.

### Gaps
- I could not source the exact bottom tab-bar structures of the Nike, Sephora or IKEA apps from an independent 2025–2026 source. Commonly observed 4–5 tab layouts, such as Nike's Home / Shop / Favourites / Bag / Profile, are unverified here.
- No source on gesture patterns (swipe-to-dismiss, swipe galleries) in these apps.

## 7. Visual design, performance and accessibility

### Takeaway
Restraint is supported by research mainly in negative form: avoid aggressive ads, pop-ups and fast or auto-rotating carousels, and give clear hit areas and scope labels. Performance has measurable conversion effects (Core Web Vitals targets: LCP ≤2.5 s, INP ≤200 ms, CLS ≤0.1), but many widely quoted figures come from vendors or are old.

### Cited Findings
- **Restraint, in negative form:**
  - Ads in prime locations and pop-up overlays provoke negative reactions; LEGO is praised for keeping prime areas for discovery.
  - Carousel timing rules apply (5–7 s on desktop, no auto-rotation on mobile).
  - Hit areas should be clear, using borders, separators, arrows and hover states.
  
  — [Baymard Nav 2025](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice)
- **Nike app aesthetic:** almost every element in black, white and grey, a minimalist palette (weaker secondary source). — [screensdesign](https://screensdesign.com/apps/ikea/)
- **Core Web Vitals:**
  - Thresholds at the 75th percentile of real visits: LCP ≤2.5 s, INP ≤200 ms, CLS ≤0.1.
  - CrUX data cited by an aggregator: 55.7% of origins pass all three; 57.1% on desktop against 49.7% on mobile.
  
  — [shno.co CWV statistics (aggregator)](https://shno.co/marketing-statistics/core-web-vitals-statistics)
- A Deloitte and Google study found a 0.1 s mobile speed improvement raised retail conversions 8.4%. This is the "Milliseconds Make Millions" study from 2020, cited here via an aggregator. — [shno.co (aggregator)](https://shno.co/marketing-statistics/core-web-vitals-statistics)
- Claims that each extra second of LCP cuts conversion 11.4% and that INP over 200 ms cuts it 23% come from a 2026 marketing-agency analysis with unclear method. Treat them as low-confidence. — [Visionary Marketing (agency)](https://visionary-marketing.co.uk/blog/core-web-vitals-ecommerce)
- Gymshark's October 2024 redesign moved to a MACH/headless architecture for faster page loads, personalisation and gradual feature rollouts (agency case page, weak evidence). — [Blackbird case page](https://www.blackbird.it.com/cases/gym-shark-case-study)

### Inferences
- "Simple and smart" in practice means:
  - one accent colour on a neutral palette
  - large product imagery
  - no pop-ups on entry
  - static or user-controlled carousels
  - delivery and returns facts placed where decisions happen
  - fast LCP from optimised hero and product images

### Gaps
- I found no 2025–2026 independent accessibility audits (WCAG) of these specific brand sites.
- No independent typography or whitespace analyses of Apple, Allbirds or Glossier from design press in 2025–2026 were retrieved.
- No CrUX data for the specific brands was retrieved.

## 8. What independent reviewers specifically praise or criticise (by brand)

### Takeaway
Among the brands in scope, Baymard's free 2025–2026 material names these:
- **Praised:** Allbirds (clickable dropdown headers), Apple (delivery dates), IKEA UK (all fulfilment options together), Away (specific error messages).
- **Criticised:** Nike (category page hierarchy), Apple (unexplained phone field), Adidas (forced account to save), Madewell and Everlane (fulfilment, model images, field marking).

Award juries were not a productive source for single-brand commerce in 2025.

### Cited Findings
- **Praised:** Allbirds (clickable "Shoes" dropdown header) — [Baymard Nav](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice); Apple (actual delivery date), IKEA UK (fulfilment options), Away (adaptive error messages), Dollar Shave Club (simple password rules), Etsy (prominent guest checkout) — [Baymard Checkout](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-UX); LEGO (restrained homepage ads), Marks & Spencer (current-scope highlighting) — [Baymard Nav](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice)
