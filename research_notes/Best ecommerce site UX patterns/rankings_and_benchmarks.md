# Best-rated ecommerce sites and apps for design and UX: rankings and benchmarks (2025–2026 evidence)

Research date: 2026-10-12. Evidence weighting: Baymard (independent, manual audits of 500+ guidelines per site) and ACSI/Forrester (customer surveys) are rated high. Similarweb is a traffic vendor; its own report is fine for traffic numbers. Webby/Apple/Google awards are editorial juries, rated medium. Listicles and aggregators are rated low and flagged as such.

Important caveat for the report writer: none of these sources rank "best ecommerce UX overall" across marketplaces and brand stores in a single list. Baymard is the only one that scores UX directly, and it gives awards by industry and by theme (search, product page, checkout and so on). ACSI and Forrester measure overall satisfaction (price, delivery and service as well as the website). Similarweb measures popularity only. The shortlist below joins these signals together; it is not one source's ranking.

## Q1. What Baymard's UX benchmarks say: who leads, and the most common failures

### Takeaway
Most large US and European sites are still "mediocre" on UX in Baymard's 2025–2026 benchmarks: about 63–64% on checkout, 52–64% on product pages, 56% on search and 75% on mobile web. The 51 Baymard UX Award winners for 2025 are mostly single-brand or specialist retailers (Adidas, Nike, Nordstrom, Chewy, Hoka, Everlane, Away, Ray-Ban, zooplus, bol). The big general marketplaces (Amazon, Walmart, eBay, Etsy, Temu) are not among the top-1% winners. Among marketplaces, only Chrono24 and Nordstrom hold Baymard's "Top 1%" label.

**Checkout (updated 25 Nov 2025)**
- Mediocre or worse checkout UX: **64%** of desktop sites, **63%** of mobile sites and **46%** of apps. No site was rated "perfect". — [Baymard Checkout UX 2025](https://baymard.com/research-articles/current-state-of-checkout-ux)
- About **70%** of shoppers abandon after adding to cart, and **18%** abandon because they don't want to create an account. Large sites could gain up to **35%** in conversion from checkout design changes. — [Baymard Checkout UX 2025](https://baymard.com/research-articles/current-state-of-checkout-ux)
- Most common checkout failures (share of sites not following the guideline): adaptive error messages 94%, quantity selector buttons 97%, delivery cutoff shown as a countdown 83%, complex password requirements 65%, guest checkout not prominent 62%, required and optional fields not both marked 61%, fulfillment options not visible 52%, no explanation of why a phone number is needed 49%, no delivery date shown 48%. — [Baymard Checkout UX 2025](https://baymard.com/research-articles/current-state-of-checkout-ux)
- The data comes from 41,000+ checkout performance scores and 33,000+ best and worst practice examples from leading US and EU sites. — [Baymard Checkout UX 2025](https://baymard.com/research-articles/current-state-of-checkout-ux)

**Product page (updated 18 Mar 2026)**
- Mediocre or worse product page UX: **52%** on desktop, **62%** on mobile and **64%** of apps. — [Baymard Product Page UX 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- Top failures: 89% handle save/wishlist poorly, 89% don't reply to negative reviews, 81% leave out price per unit, 78% show no gifting options, 67% give no estimate of total order cost, 63% don't let users browse through review images, 57% use dropdowns instead of buttons for size, 44% don't link the return policy, 37% lack "in scale" images, 23% have no images on a human model. — [Baymard Product Page UX 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- Good examples Baymard names: Marks & Spencer (size buttons), GoPro (in-scale images), BonPrix (wishlist for guests), The Vitamin Shoppe (price per serving), **Etsy (gifting options)**, Lamps Plus (review image carousel). — [Baymard Product Page UX 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- Older figure for comparison: a search snippet gave "44% mediocre or worse" and another gave "51%" for an earlier product page benchmark. The underlying pages were not opened, so treat both as superseded by the 2026 numbers. — [Baymard product page research](https://baymard.com/product-page)

**On-site search (Apr 2026)**
- **56%** of sites have mediocre or worse search UX (2026 Search UX benchmark, "8 Search Query Types" article dated 29 Apr 2026). — [Baymard on-site search collection](https://baymard.com/blog/collections/on-site-search)
- 69% of sites don't give relevant autocomplete suggestions for close misspellings. 80% offer autocomplete, but only 19% of those reach the top performance level. 46% handle guidance to category scopes badly. 37% don't keep the user's query after the search. 21% have no search submit button on mobile. — [Baymard on-site search collection](https://baymard.com/blog/collections/on-site-search)

**Mobile web (updated 14 Jul 2026)**
- 150+ mobile sites and 71,000+ UX elements reviewed by hand. **75%** of sites are rated "Mediocre" and 95% are "Mediocre" or "Decent". Only **1** site is rated "Good" and none "Perfect". Mobile is about three-quarters of ecommerce traffic. — [Baymard Mobile UX 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/mobile-ux-ecommerce)
- Top mobile failures: 94% don't highlight the current scope in navigation, 93% show generic validation errors, 73% don't show color swatches in full, 66% don't show applied filters, 57% don't let users browse through review images, 54% have no address validator, 42% list product variations as separate items, 42% have no "View All" option in categories, 28% have no autocomplete for misspelled terms, 27% have no search submit button. — [Baymard Mobile UX 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/mobile-ux-ecommerce)
- Homepage and category navigation: up to **67%** of leading US and EU sites are "mediocre" to "poor" (from a search snippet of Baymard's mobile collection; date not confirmed). — [Baymard mobile collection](https://baymard.com/blog/collections/mobile)

### Inferences
- In Baymard's scoring, the strongest UX comes from focused vertical or brand retailers (apparel and footwear: Adidas, Nike, Nordstrom, Hoka, Levi's, Everlane; pets: Chewy, zooplus; eyewear: Ray-Ban, Oakley) and from a few European specialists (bol, Gamma, Netonnet, RTV Euro AGD). It does not come from the largest general marketplaces.
- For multi-seller marketplace models, the Baymard-backed names are **bol.com** (search), **Chrono24** (overall top 1%), **Nordstrom** (top 1%; marketplace plus first-party) and **Etsy** (gifting on product pages). Amazon wins on popularity and satisfaction (see Q3), not on Baymard awards.
- Apps do better than web on checkout (46% mediocre against 63–64%) but worse on product pages (64% mediocre). This suggests apps simplify paying but often cut product information.

### Gaps
- Baymard's per-site scores (for example Amazon's or IKEA's rank among the 344) sit in interactive charts and paid content, so they could not be extracted.
- Baymard does not benchmark Asian or LatAm marketplaces (Shopee, Mercado Libre, Rakuten, Temu, AliExpress) in its public data. No independent UX score was found for them.
- Baymard's cart abandonment figure of about 70% comes from its checkout article. The detailed 2025 list of abandonment reasons was not extracted, apart from the 18% for account creation.

## Q2. Design and UX awards (Webby, Awwwards, Apple, Google Play)

### Takeaway
Design awards in 2025–2026 barely recognise mainstream shopping sites. Apple's and Google Play's 2025 top picks included no shopping apps. Webby Shopping winners go to Apple Pay (2026) and grocery apps (Albertsons, 2024). Awwwards e-commerce honours go to small, visually striking DTC brand sites (Oura Ring and others) rather than high-traffic stores. Awards are weak evidence for "simple UX at scale".

### Cited Findings
- **2026 Webby Awards**: **Apple Pay** won both the Webby and the People's Voice award in Shopping & Retail, Consumer Apps (Apps, Software & Immersive). — [MacTech, Apr 2026](https://www.mactech.com/2026/04/21/apple-wins-three-webby-awards-from-the-international-academy-of-digital-arts-sciences/amp/); [Apple World Today, Apr 2026](https://appleworld.today/2026/04/apple-wins-three-webby-awards-from-the-international-academy-of-digital-arts-sciences/)

It does not list Shopping & Retail category winners; the full directory at webbyawards.com/winners would be needed. — [Webby 2025 press release](https://www.webbyawards.com/press/press-releases/29th-annual-webby-awards-announce-2025-winners/)
- 2025 Webby nominee: Shoplazza (a commerce platform) for Best E-Commerce, Finance & Banking Apps & Software (nomination only; from a press release, low weight). — [Bradford Era, Apr 2025](https://bradfordera.com/2025/04/23/shoplazza-nominated-for-best-e-commerce-finance-banking-apps-software-at-the-29th-annual-webby-awards)
- **2024 Webby** (older): Albertsons won the People's Voice award for best Shopping & Retail app. Note that Albertsons and Safeway also won Baymard search awards in 2025. — [Business Wire, Apr 2024](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240430367531/en/Albertsons-Companies-Wins-%E2%80%98People%E2%80%99s-Voice%E2%80%99-Webby-Award-for-Best-Shopping-and-Retail-App/)

- **Awwwards e-commerce honours, 2025**: Oura Ring, SPYLT Milk, Telepathic Instruments, AEVI, Gielly Green, LAB46, CANCAN Furnishings, BOMBON Royal Swedish Candy and others. ### Inferences
- Awwwards and Webby reward visual craft and brand storytelling, which suits single-brand stores (Apple, Nike and DTC brands). Baymard rewards task efficiency. These measure different things, and the report should not treat them as interchangeable.
- Apple's own properties (Apple Pay, Apple Cash in 2025) keep winning Webbys. That backs Apple as a design reference for payment and checkout flows, but not as a Baymard-scored retailer.

### Gaps
- The full lists of 2025 and 2026 Webby Shopping winners and nominees (websites and apps) were not retrieved. The Webby site directory is needed for these.
- No 2025–2026 Red Dot, iF or CSS Design Awards results for ecommerce were checked; there was not enough tool budget.
- Apple Design Awards (WWDC, June) for 2025–2026 were not checked separately from the App Store Awards. Neither is known to have included a shopping app.

## Q3. Customer satisfaction and usability rankings (ACSI, Forrester, NN/g, app store ratings)

### Takeaway
Among large US online retailers, Amazon and Chewy lead ACSI (2025: Chewy 85, Amazon 83; 2026: Amazon, Chewy and Nordstrom tied at 82, though the 2026 figures come from a secondary source). Temu is the lowest of the big players (75 in 2025, 74 in 2026). Forrester's 2025 CX Index names Chewy and Zappos.com as "elite" brands. Chewy is the one name that is top-rated by Baymard, ACSI and Forrester at once.

### Cited Findings
**ACSI Retail and Consumer Shipping Study 2025 (released 28 Jan 2025)**
- Online retail average **79** (down 1 point). 65%+ of tracked online brands declined. **Chewy 85** (+1) was top, then **Amazon 83** (unchanged; first among "online multimarket" retailers), Home Depot.com 82 (+3), **eBay 81**, Kohl's.com 79, **Etsy 78** (−1), Costco.com 77 (−2), and **Temu 75** (new). — [Homepage News, 28 Jan 2025](https://www.homepagenews.com/retail-articles/etailer-consumer-satisfaction-slips-as-general-merchandisers-gain-slightly); [ACSI 2025 press release](https://theacsi.com/news-and-resources/press-releases/2025/01/28/press-release-retail-and-consumer-shipping-study-2025/); [ACSI 2025 study PDF](https://theacsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/25jan_Retail-Study-FINAL.pdf)
- Drivers: "mobile shopping capabilities, website quality, and perceptions of value" matter more and more to younger shoppers (18–25), whose satisfaction is notably lower. — [Homepage News, 28 Jan 2025](https://www.homepagenews.com/retail-articles/etailer-consumer-satisfaction-slips-as-general-merchandisers-gain-slightly)
- Amazon Shipping was new to the study and led delivery companies with 81. Consumer shipping overall was 77, with mobile app experience slightly down. — [ACSI 2025 press release](https://theacsi.com/news-and-resources/press-releases/2025/01/28/press-release-retail-and-consumer-shipping-study-2025/) (via search summary)

**ACSI 2026 (released 27 Jan 2026)**
- Online retail average **79**, stable. **Amazon, Nordstrom and Chewy tied at 82**. eBay 78, Etsy 77, Temu 74. These figures come from a search summary of a Clark.com article. **NN/g**

NN/g describes five shopper types: product-focused, browsers, researchers, bargain hunters and one-time shoppers. Shoppers use product pages to answer specific questions. NN/g does not publish a ranking of sites. — [NN/g Ecommerce UX report](https://www.nngroup.com/reports/ecommerce-user-experience/); [NN/g 5 shopper types](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/5-types-e-commerce-shoppers/)

**App store ratings and downloads**
- Temu and Shein are both rated **4.7/5** on the App Store, with about 2.2M ratings for Temu and about 3.6M for Shein. This is from a comparison site and low weight; ratings change over time. — [unstar.app comparison](https://unstar.app/de/compare/temu-vs-shein) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- High app store ratings for Temu and Shein (4.7) sit alongside the lowest ACSI score among major online retailers (Temu 74–75). App store stars, often prompted inside the app around rewards, are a poor measure of UX quality, and the report should treat them with caution.
- Chewy is the clearest case where independent UX audits (Baymard top 1%), satisfaction (ACSI #1 in 2025) and CX (Forrester elite) all agree. Amazon is the clearest case of high satisfaction and high popularity without Baymard UX awards.

### Gaps
- The primary ACSI 2026 numbers and the full brand list (for example Walmart.com, Target.com, Apple, Nike, Shein, Wayfair) were not verified because of 403 errors.
- No current (2025–2026) primary App Store or Play ratings and review counts were collected for Amazon, Etsy, eBay, Zalando, Mercado Libre, Shopee, IKEA, Nike or Sephora.
- No Forrester per-brand retail scores were found; they are paywalled.

On apps, Amazon leads with about 652M monthly active users, followed by Shopee (about 393M) and Temu (about 246M). Growth is moving to apps (+13% sessions) and away from the web (−1% visits).

## Q5. Design qualities that explain high rankings

### Takeaway
What separates the leaders, judging by where the majority of sites fail, is not visual flair. It is getting the fundamentals right: guest checkout and low form friction, clear errors and address validation, search that handles misspellings and keeps the query, visible applied filters and scope, size buttons and full color swatches, total cost and delivery date shown early, review images users can browse, and good mobile navigation. Satisfaction leaders (Chewy, Amazon) add reliable delivery and service on top.

### Cited Findings
- **Checkout friction** is the largest measurable lever: up to a 35% conversion gain, and 18% of shoppers abandon over forced account creation. 62% of sites don't make guest checkout prominent, 65% have complex password rules and 94% lack adaptive error messages. — [Baymard Checkout UX 2025](https://baymard.com/research-articles/current-state-of-checkout-ux)
- **Delivery transparency**: 48% of sites don't show a delivery date, 83% don't show a cutoff countdown, and 67% of product pages don't estimate total cost. — [Baymard Checkout UX 2025](https://baymard.com/research-articles/current-state-of-checkout-ux); [Baymard Product Page UX 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- **Search quality**: 69% fail on close misspellings in autocomplete, 46% fail on scope guidance and 37% don't keep the query. Baymard search award winners: bol, Gamma, Albertsons, Safeway. — [Baymard on-site search](https://baymard.com/blog/collections/on-site-search); [Baymard UX Awards 2025](https://baymard.com/research-articles/ux-awards-2025)
- **Filtering and navigation clarity on mobile**: 94% don't highlight the current scope, 66% don't show applied filters, and 42% list variations as separate products. — [Baymard Mobile UX 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/mobile-ux-ecommerce)
- **Trust signals on product pages**: 44% don't link the return policy, 89% don't reply to negative reviews, and 63% don't let users browse through review images. — [Baymard Product Page UX 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- **Post-purchase**: Nike won Baymard's 2025 award for Order Tracking & Returns, and GoPro and Nisbets won for Customer Accounts. These show that leaders design the account and returns flows too, not only the path to purchase. — [Baymard UX Awards 2025](https://baymard.com/research-articles/ux-awards-2025)
- **Value, mobile and website quality** drive satisfaction among younger shoppers (ACSI 2025). — [Homepage News](https://www.homepagenews.com/retail-articles/etailer-consumer-satisfaction-slips-as-general-merchandisers-gain-slightly)
- **Apps and AI shopping**: app sessions +13% against web −1%, and ChatGPT referrals convert at 11.4% against 5.3% for organic search. App-first UX and readiness for AI discovery are emerging differentiators. — [Similarweb 2025](https://ir.similarweb.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/132)

### Inferences
**Draft shortlist, combining Baymard (UX), ACSI/Forrester (satisfaction) and Similarweb (popularity):**

*Multi-seller marketplaces*
- **Amazon**: #1 by traffic and app use (Similarweb 2025) and ACSI #1–2 (83 in 2025, 82 in 2026 per the secondary source). It holds no Baymard top-1% award. Strengths are mainly delivery, selection and familiarity.
- **bol.com (NL)**: Baymard 2025 award for On-Site Search on Desktop and Mobile. It is the best Baymard-backed European marketplace.
- **Nordstrom** (first-party plus marketplace): Baymard 2025 award (apparel) and marketplace "Top 1%", and ACSI 82 in 2026 (secondary source). It is the strongest cross-validated "marketplace-ish" pick.
- **Chrono24** (watch marketplace): Baymard marketplace "Top 1%" and 2025 award winner.
- **Etsy**: ACSI 78 in 2025 and 77 in 2026 (secondary source), and a Baymard positive example for gifting options on product pages.
